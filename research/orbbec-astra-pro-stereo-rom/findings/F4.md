# F4: 2D-to-3D pose lifting using depth

## Findings

### [1] SimpleDepthPose performs 2D-to-3D pose lifting by directly looking up depth values at 2D keypoint pixel locations from an aligned depth image, using a cross-shaped median filter and per-joint depth offsets (e.g., +3cm for shoulders), requiring no training and achieving state-of-the-art multi-view accuracy on MVOR (PCP 74.0%, MPJPE 113mm) and Panoptic (PCP 96.9%, MPJPE 45.5mm).
- quote: "Extract the distance of each detected visible joint from the aligned depth images... The depth values are selected using two rectangular cutouts that form a cross-shape, to emphasize the center region and reduce outliers... a static per-joint offset is added to the distance, depending on the type of the joint, because a depth camera normally measures the distance to the surface, but the target location is the center of the joint. These offsets are estimated using normal human proportions. For example, 3cm are added for shoulders and knees, or 1cm for the wrists."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18478
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-01-30 (v1), 2025-03-07 (v2)
- confidence: high

### [2] SimpleDepthPose's depth-map-to-3D pipeline is highly relevant to the Orbbec Astra Pro workflow: it uses any off-the-shelf 2D pose estimator (HigherHrNet, RTMPose) on the RGB image, then extracts depth at each keypoint from the aligned depth image, converts to 3D via camera intrinsics/extrinsics, fuses multi-view proposals, and runs in ~3ms per frame (2.6ms depth extraction + 0.4ms fusion).
- quote: "Due to the use of depth information from the cameras, the (learned) triangulation step can be skipped, and the algorithm is very fast. On Panoptic the average time is about 2.6ms for the depth extraction and 0.4ms for multi-view fusion."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18478
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-01-30
- confidence: high

### [3] SimpleDepthPose demonstrates that using depth data significantly increases detection rates of persons and keypoints, especially under heavy occlusion, even if joint localization accuracy does not always improve over learned triangulation methods: "the use of depth data does not necessarily lead to more accurate results in terms of joint localization, but can significantly increase the number of detected keypoints and persons, especially in strongly occluded settings."
- quote: "In this work, we present a novel algorithm that excels in multi-view, multi-person pose estimation by incorporating depth information... the use of depth data does not necessarily lead to more accurate results in terms of joint localization, but can significantly increase the number of detected keypoints and persons, especially in strongly occluded settings."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18478
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-01-30
- confidence: high

### [4] AugLift (Warner et al., 2025) enriches standard 2D-to-3D pose lifting by adding an Uncertainty-Aware Depth Descriptor (UADD) -- a compact 4-tuple (confidence, depth_median, depth_min, depth_max) per keypoint from a monocular depth map -- expanding the input from 2D to 6D per joint, reducing cross-dataset MPJPE by 10.1% on average across 4 datasets and 4 lifting architectures, and achieving SOTA when combined with PoseAug (62.4mm on 3DHP, 92.6mm on 3DPW).
- quote: "We propose AugLift, which changes the representation format of lifting from 2D coordinates to a 6D geometric descriptor via two modules: (1) an Uncertainty-Aware Depth Descriptor (UADD)—a compact tuple (c,d,d_min,d_max) extracted from a confidence-scaled neighborhood of an off-the-shelf monocular depth map... In the detection setting, AugLift reduces cross-dataset MPJPE by 10.1% on average across four datasets and four lifting architectures while improving in-distribution accuracy by 4.0%."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07112
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-08-09 (v1), 2026-04-07 (v4)
- confidence: high

### [5] AugLift's UADD design uses confidence-aware radius for depth sampling: low-confidence (occluded) keypoints use a wider sampling radius in the depth map, while high-confidence keypoints use a tight radius, explicitly handling the challenge that monocular depth only provides a lower bound for occluded joint depth.
- quote: "Given keypoints from a 2D detector and a depth map D from an off-the-shelf MDE, we define a confidence-aware radius r_j = r_min + (1 - c_j)(r_max - r_min) for each joint. Within the circular neighborhood N_j of radius r_j, we compute three depth statistics—median d_j, minimum d_j^min, maximum d_j^max—forming the UADD... The key intuition is that d_j^min acts as a geometric lower bound on joint depth (the nearest visible surface), while confidence controls the neighborhood size: tight for visible joints, broad for occluded ones."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07112
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-08-09
- confidence: high

### [6] AugLift is composable with any existing lifting architecture (PoseFormer, MotionBERT, VideoPose3D, SimpleBaseline) -- it operates purely at the representation level by widening the input layer from 2K to 6K channels, requiring no architectural changes and no retraining of the depth estimator. This means it could directly enhance RTMPose+MotionAGFormer pipelines.
- quote: "AugLift requires no new sensors, no new data collection, and no architectural changes beyond widening the input layer; because it operates at the representation level, it is composable with any lifting architecture or domain generalization technique. Integration requires only widening the input layer from 2K to 6K channels; all other layers, losses, and training remain unchanged."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07112
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-08-09
- confidence: high

### [7] MotionAGFormer (Mehraban et al., WACV 2024) is a state-of-the-art 2D-to-3D lifting architecture using a parallel transformer + GCNFormer design, achieving 38.4mm P1 error on Human3.6M and 16.2mm on MPI-INF-3DHP, while using a quarter of the parameters and 3x less computation than prior SOTA. It operates on monocular 2D keypoint sequences as input (no depth), making it a candidate lifter that could benefit from AugLift-style depth enrichment.
- quote: "We present a novel Attention-GCNFormer (AGFormer) block that divides the number of channels by using two parallel transformer and GCNFormer streams... MotionAGFormer-B achieves state-of-the-art results, with P1 errors of 38.4mm and 16.2mm, respectively. Remarkably, it uses a quarter of the parameters and is three times more computationally efficient than the previous leading model on Human3.6M dataset."
- url: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/html/Mehraban_MotionAGFormer_Enhancing_3D_Human_Pose_Estimation_With_a_Transformer-GCNFormer_Network_WACV_2024_paper.html
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-01 (WACV 2024)
- confidence: high

### [8] The depth-map lookup approach (reading depth at 2D keypoint locations from an aligned RGB-D sensor) is the simplest and most direct 2D-to-3D lifting strategy for depth cameras like the Orbbec Astra Pro. OpenPTrack, an established robotics framework, uses this exact pipeline: compute 2D keypoints from RGB, look up depth at those pixel coordinates, and deproject to 3D using camera intrinsics. SimpleDepthPose improves on OpenPTrack with cross-shaped median filtering and per-joint offsets.
- quote: "OpenPTrack, which is frequently employed in robotics, initially calculates the 2D keypoints for each image, and then leverages depth images to determine the distance of each joint to the cameras. It generates a 3D person proposal from each view... SimpleDepthPose... Two key differences are to account for the joint visibility and the improved concept of depth extraction."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18478
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-01-30
- confidence: high

### [9] Oracle experiments in AugLift show that even coarse 3-bin ordinal depth information reduces cross-dataset error (H3.6M->3DPW) by approximately 25%, suggesting that providing the lifter with any depth signal -- whether from a real depth sensor (like Orbbec) or monocular depth estimation -- provides substantial geometric disambiguation.
- quote: "To estimate their upper bound, we conducted oracle experiments using privileged ground-truth ordinal depth information at varying granularities. The results were compelling: augmenting the sparse (x,y) input with even coarse, three-bin ordinal depth information reduced cross-dataset error (H3.6M->3DPW) by approximately 25%."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07112
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-08-09
- confidence: high

### [10] The depth-guided feature lifting taxonomy (per EmergentMind, updated April 2026) identifies at least six distinct strategies for incorporating depth into 2D-to-3D lifting: per-joint concatenation (AugLift), voxel expansion (DFA3D), point painting (LVIC), Gaussian splatting (FisheyeGaussianLift), cascaded volumetric regression, and MLP+attention fusion (LiftFeat). For a shoulder ROM pipeline with a real depth sensor, the per-joint concatenation strategy (AugLift) or direct depth-map lookup (SimpleDepthPose) are the most practical.
- quote: "Depth-guided feature lifting refers to a class of techniques that leverage depth or geometric signals—estimated or measured—to enhance the lifting of features from 2D observations (images or keypoints) into 3D representations... Methodological Taxonomy: 2D Descriptor Lifting with 3D Cues, Dense Feature Expansion via Depth Distributions, Per-Point Visual-LiDAR Fusion, Keypoint-Augmented Lifting, Gaussian Feature Fields and Uncertainty Integration, Cascaded Lifting for Pose Estimation."
- url: https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/depth-guided-feature-lifting
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-04-01 (updated)
- confidence: high
