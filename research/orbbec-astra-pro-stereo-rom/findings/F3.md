# F3: Depth-enhanced 2D pose

## Findings

### [1] Zimmermann et al. (ICRA 2018) showed that fusing a 2D keypoint detector (OpenPose) with depth-map voxelization via VoxelPoseNet achieves 11.2 cm average 3D keypoint error vs 22.7 cm for monocular-only methods on the Captury dataset, demonstrating that depth disambiguates left/right and resolves occlusion.
- quote: "color information helps to disambiguate left and right side, which is infeasible from depth alone. On the other hand, the depth map provides valuable information to exactly infer the 3D keypoint. Furthermore, the network learns a prior about possible body part configurations, which makes it possible to infer 3D locations even for completely occluded keypoints."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02622
- source_type: primary
- published: 2018-03-07 (ICRA 2018)
- confidence: high

### [2] Zimmermann et al. showed that a naive depth-lifting baseline (picking the median depth from a 3x3 neighborhood around the 2D keypoint) achieves reasonable but error-prone results because "any kind of occlusion results into an error," while their learned VoxelPoseNet approach using tiled 2D score maps along the z-axis with a 3D occupancy grid outperforms it by learning to handle occlusion and noisy depth.
- quote: "The baseline named Naive Lifting uses the same Keypoint detector for color images as our proposed approach and simply picks the corresponding depth value from the depth map. It chooses the depth value as median value of the 3×3 closest neighbors. The approach shows reasonable performance, but is prone to pick bad depth values from the noisy depth map."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/1803.02622v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2018-03-07
- confidence: high

### [3] Liu et al. (AAAI 2024) proposed Keypoint-Fusion for RGB-D hand pose estimation, which performs cross-modal fusion at sparse keypoint locations rather than dense pixel-wise fusion, with "depth modality provides 3D geometric information for RGB feature pixels, and RGB modality complements the precise edge information lost due to the depth noise," achieving state-of-the-art on two challenging hand pose datasets.
- quote: "By explicitly extracting relatively more reliable information as disambiguation evidence, depth modality provides 3D geometric information for RGB feature pixels, and RGB modality complements the precise edge information lost due to the depth noise. Keypoint-Fusion achieves state-of-the-art performance on two challenging hand datasets, significantly decreasing the error compared with previous single-modal methods."
- url: https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/28166
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-03-24 (AAAI 2024)
- confidence: high

### [4] Hardy & Kim (WACV 2024) presented LInKs ("Lifting Independent Keypoints"), which showed that a lift-then-fill approach (lifting occluded 2D pose to 3D first, then inferring occluded parts in 3D space) significantly outperforms methods that complete the pose in 2D space alone, achieving a 7.9% reduction in reconstruction error on Human3.6M compared to prior works.
- quote: "Our results consistently demonstrate the superiority of handling all types of occlusions in 3D space when compared to others that complete the pose in 2D space. Our approach also exhibits consistent accuracy in scenarios without occlusion, as evidenced by a 7.9% reduction in reconstruction error compared to prior works on the Human3.6M dataset."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07243
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-09-13 (WACV 2024)
- confidence: high

### [5] LInKs also demonstrated that lifting independent body-part subsets of the 2D skeleton separately (rather than the whole skeleton at once) reduces reconstruction error even without occlusion, attributed to "the reduction of long-range keypoint correlations" between distant joints.
- quote: "we are the first to investigate if different parts of the 2D kinematic skeleton can be lifted independently which we find by itself reduces the error of current lifting approaches. We attribute this to the reduction of long-range keypoint correlations."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07243
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-09-13
- confidence: high

### [6] Shotton et al. (CACM 2013, Microsoft) established the foundational approach for depth-only pose estimation using per-pixel body-part classification on single depth images, with the classifier designed to "learn to disambiguate the left and right sides of the body" using synthetic training data; this work underpinned Kinect SDK's body tracking and has 2,480+ citations.
- quote: "By designing an intermediate representation in terms of body parts, the difficult pose estimation problem is transformed into a simpler per-pixel classification problem, for which efficient machine learning techniques exist... the classifier to learn to disambiguate the left and right sides"
- url: https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/2398356.2398381
- source_type: primary
- published: 2013 (CACM)
- confidence: high

### [7] Khanghah et al. (BioMed Eng Online, 2024) demonstrated that LiDAR depth on iPad enables accurate shoulder abduction angle measurement beyond what 2D pose systems can achieve, finding "LiDAR's depth feature enabled accurate computation of in-depth angles" -- directly relevant because depth resolves the out-of-plane shoulder rotation that 2D systems miss.
- quote: "LiDAR's depth feature enabled accurate computation of in-depth angles beyond... rehab, our study points toward a potential avenue for automating range of motion assessments in clinical"
- url: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12938-024-01203-5
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024
- confidence: high

### [8] Lauman et al. (Sensors, 2025) validated a depth sensor-based augmented rehabilitation system (MARS) for shoulder and elbow ROM, noting its "2D framework restricts multiplanar analysis and depth," which is a direct limitation when measuring shoulder movements that involve out-of-plane rotation -- motivating the need for 3D-aware pose enhancement.
- quote: "a depth sensor-based device with body-mapping capabilities... and its 2D framework restricts multiplanar analysis and depth"
- url: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/14/4269
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025
- confidence: high

### [9] van den Hoorn et al. (Sensors, 2025) published a narrative review on clinical shoulder ROM assessment highlighting that "depth sensor techniques commonly used for pose estimation" have a critical role "in ensuring accurate estimations of shoulder range of motion," confirming the clinical relevance of depth-enhanced pose estimation for ROM measurement.
- quote: "depth sensor techniques commonly used for pose estimation... in ensuring accurate estimations of shoulder range of motion"
- url: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/3/667
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025
- confidence: medium

### [10] Zimmermann's Kinect SDK evaluation revealed that the SDK "is unable to distinguish if a person is facing towards or away from the camera, which expresses itself in mixing up left and right side," demonstrating a concrete failure mode of depth-only body trackers that color+depth fusion resolves -- the same left/right confusion risk applies to any depth-enhanced 2D pose pipeline if color information is not used for disambiguation.
- quote: "qualitative examples reveal that the SDK is led astray by objects and is unable to distinguish if a person is facing towards or away from the camera, which expresses itself in mixing up left and right side."
- url: https://arxiv.org/html/1803.02622v2
- source_type: primary
- published: 2018-03-07
- confidence: high

### [11] Zhang et al. (Science China Technological Sciences, 2026) proposed a fine-grained rehabilitation assessment system that "integrate the depth camera and pose estimation algorithms" to assess rehabilitation training tasks, confirming the trend toward depth-camera-enhanced pose pipelines for clinical rehabilitation measurement.
- quote: "integrate the depth camera and pose estimation algorithms... revolutionize the existing clinical rehabilitation paradigm"
- url: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11431-026-3364-3
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026
- confidence: medium
