# F7: Accuracy benchmarks

## Findings

### [1] Meta-analysis of 15 studies shows digital shoulder ROM devices have pooled mean bias of -0.25 degrees vs goniometry, with no significant difference between infrared/RGB-D and 3D motion analysis systems (p=0.83)
- quote: "Pooled mean bias was -0.25 degrees (-1.25, 0.75 95% LOA, random effects model) overall. This did not differ by device type (p = 0.83), sensor or non-sensor-based devices (p = 0.62) or plane of movement (p = 0.91)."
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11418675/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-08-31
- confidence: high

### [2] Kinect V2 for shoulder ROM achieves LOA within ±10 degrees (clinically acceptable) for flexion, abduction, external rotation, and internal rotation vs Vicon marker-based motion capture
- quote: "Zulkarnain et al. (2017) — Flexion LOA: -8.4 to 7.3; Abduction LOA: -7.1 to 7.8; External rotation (elbow flexed) LOA: -8.0 to 7.4; Internal rotation (elbow flexed) LOA: -8.1 to 3.6."
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11418675/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-08-31
- confidence: high

### [3] Kinect V2 shoulder ROM vs goniometer shows mean bias of -2.83 degrees for flexion (LOA -9.88 to 4.23) and 0.33 degrees for abduction (LOA -4.86 to 5.51), but internal rotation has wider LOA of -21.42 to 8.07 degrees
- quote: "Cubukcu 2020 — Flexion: -2.83 (-9.88, 4.23); Extension: -0.1 (-1.69, 1.49); Abduction: 0.33 (-4.86, 5.51); External rotation: -0.5 (-7.55, 6.55); Internal rotation: -6.67 (-21.42, 8.07)."
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11418675/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-08-31
- confidence: high

### [4] RGB-D camera + AI (Intel RealSense D435) for shoulder ROM achieves ICC 0.97 for flexion, 0.98 for abduction, but only 0.82 for external rotation; mean bias ranges from -1.14 to 7.5 degrees across planes
- quote: "Gauci 2022 — Flexion ICC 0.97 (0.95-0.98), bias -1.14, LOA -17.6 to 15.3; Abduction ICC 0.98 (0.96-0.99), bias -0.33, LOA -14.6 to 13.9; External rotation ICC 0.82 (0.72-0.89), bias 7.5, LOA -17.9 to 32.9."
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11418675/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-08-31
- confidence: high

### [5] Systematic review of 14 studies finds RGB-D sensors (Kinect V2, Azure Kinect, RealSense) show good-to-excellent validity for simple shoulder movements (flexion, abduction) but inconsistent results for complex/rotational movements
- quote: "The findings indicate that intra-rater reliability findings across six studies were inconsistent, with simple movements like abduction and flexion demonstrating better reliability and less heterogeneity compared to complex movements. Validity assessments across 12 studies also showed inconsistency, with abduction and flexion measurements exhibiting higher validity than rotational movements."
- url: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1570637/full
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-23
- confidence: high

### [6] Azure Kinect shows no significant reliability difference vs Kinect V2 for shoulder ROM but performs better in rotation measurements; inter-rater ICC for Azure Kinect abduction is 0.93 vs Kinect V2's 0.78
- quote: "Azure Kinect showed superior performance in rotation measurements. Notably, Azure Kinect performed better in external rotation measurements. Inter-rater ICC: Azure Kinect abduction 0.93 vs Kinect V2 0.78; Azure Kinect ER (elbow flexed) 0.79 vs Kinect V2 0.56."
- url: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1570637/full
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-23
- confidence: high

### [7] RGB-D camera (Intel L515) for lower limb joint ROM measurement achieves maximum error of 2.2 degrees in sagittal plane and 2.65 degrees in coronal plane vs angle sensors
- quote: "The angle difference in the sagittal plane measured by the proposed detection method and angle sensor is relatively conservative, and the maximum measurement error is not more than 2.2 degrees. The angle difference in the coronal plane between the angle at the peak obtained by the designed detection system and the angle sensor is not more than 2.65 degrees."
- url: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2021.753924/full
- source_type: primary
- published: 2021-10-15
- confidence: high

### [8] IMU sensor-based systems achieve the tightest LOA for shoulder ROM vs goniometry: Rigoni et al. (2019) reports ICC 0.99 for all planes with LOA of -3.2 to 3.2 degrees for flexion
- quote: "Rigoni 2019 — Flexion ICC 0.99 (0.99-0.99), bias 0, LOA -3.2 to 3.2; Abduction ICC 0.99, bias -0.8, LOA -4.5 to 2.9; External rotation ICC 0.99, bias -0.3, LOA -3.3 to 2.7; Internal rotation ICC 0.99, bias -0.9, LOA -4.2 to 2.4."
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11418675/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-08-31
- confidence: high

### [9] Kinect V2 intra-rater reliability for simple shoulder movements ranges from ICC 0.62 (extension) to 0.97 (internal rotation elbow flexed); abduction ICC 0.64-0.86, flexion ICC 0.85-0.90
- quote: "Cubukcu et al. (2020) Kinect V2 intra-rater ICC: Flexion 0.85, Extension 0.62, Abduction 0.86, ER (elbow flexed) 0.84, IR (elbow flexed) 0.97. Ozsoy et al. (2022) Kinect V2 intra-rater ICC: Flexion 0.90, Abduction 0.64, IR (elbow flexed) 0.96."
- url: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1570637/full
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-23
- confidence: high

### [10] Clinically acceptable LOA threshold of ±10 degrees is met by most RGB-D shoulder ROM studies for simple movements, but 5 of 12 validity studies report discrepancies exceeding 10 degrees
- quote: "Two studies (Cubukcu et al., 2020; Zulkarnain et al., 2017) that employed Bland-Altman plot reported clinically acceptable discrepancies, the remaining five studies (Gauci et al., 2023; Jo et al., 2022; Ozsoy et al., 2022; Faity et al., 2022; Kuster et al., 2016; Foreman and Engsberg, 2020) reported discrepancies exceeding 10 degrees."
- url: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1570637/full
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-23
- confidence: high

### [11] Van den Hoorn et al. (2026) found that 2D-pose estimation (monocular) accuracy for shoulder ROM depends on reliable 2D joint position estimates, and that depth-aware methods improve over pure 2D estimates
- quote: "2D-pose estimation offers a convenient method for assessing shoulder range-of-motion, but its accuracy compared [to 3D motion capture] accurately relies on both reliable 2D-pose position estimates."
- url: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/17585732251360746
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026
- confidence: medium

### [12] Orbbec Astra Pro is specifically named in a systematic review as one of the RGB-D sensors suitable for shoulder ROM measurement, released in 2016 alongside Kinect V2 (2013), RealSense (2017), and Azure Kinect (2019)
- quote: "Since then, several manufacturers have developed RGB-depth (RGB-D) sensors. Microsoft released Kinect V2 in 2013; Orbbec released Astra Pro (Orbbec, Troy, MI) in 2016; Intel released RealSense (Intel Corp., Santa Clara, CA) in 2017; and Microsoft subsequently launched Azure Kinect (Kinect V4) in 2019."
- url: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1570637/full
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-05-23
- confidence: high
